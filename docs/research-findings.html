The three findings that matter most

1 · Our price point is contradicted by the only real data in the category

Cora (by Every) published that their AI inference cost $25–35 per user per month. After "nearly burning six figures" and a 10× optimisation, they reached "under $5." Their optimised floor is above our $1–3 ceiling.

And the category is repricing upward in public: Shortwave's entry tier went $7 → $30 in two years (Wayback-verified), and is now monthly-only with no annual discount. The one product in our price band — SaneBox at $2–10 — does no generative AI at all.

Our architecture is genuinely different — a 22M classifier absorbs the volume, the frontier model touches ~8 drafts a day, where Cora summarises every newsletter and generates two briefs daily. But that is our arithmetic against their measurement. Treat $5 as the prior until we measure our own.

2 · The wedge is the obligation ledger, and it is header-computable

Across ~5,700 analysed comments, the most vividly described unmet need was tracking what you owe and what you are owed. Every competitor review names it as missing — absent from Superhuman, Shortwave, Fyxer, Inbox Zero and Spark.

Three facts make it ours: it computes almost entirely from headers (you sent Message-ID: X; nothing arrived with In-Reply-To: X) — so it is free, fast and private; the reply arriving is the closure signal, which fixes the exact failure that kills stars and flags ("I forget to unstar things"); and there is no published academic work on the complete job. Strong demand, silent literature.

3 · Reposition: deadline guardian, not inbox-zero tool

Two streams appeared to contradict each other. Western forums show almost no demand for deadline extraction — it was the one core feature with no user pull found on HN or Reddit. The Pakistan research shows the opposite, because a missed deadline there is not a late fee.

Under s.114B, an unmet FBR notice lets the state block your SIM, cut your electricity and gas, and bar foreign travel. ITGO No. 01 of 2024 named 506,671 individuals for SIM blocking. And the Federal Tax Ombudsman has formally ruled that FBR posting notices only to the IRIS portal, without email or SMS push, is legally insufficient — and directed them to fix it.

Our problem statement, validated by a state ombudsman. Same product, same architecture — different headline.

And one finding that validates the whole thesis

Kushlev & Dunn (2015), a two-week crossover field experiment with 124 participants, is the closest thing to direct evidence for what we are building. Participants limited to checking email three times a day — versus checking freely — showed significantly lower daily stress (d = .37), lower tension and less distraction.

Crucially: emails received and replied to did not differ between conditions. The intervention changed checking frequency, not volume. And Mark et al. (2012) found that cutting email off entirely halved task-switching — 37.1 to 18.2 window switches per hour.

That is our product thesis, measured. The win comes from removing the checking, not from processing mail faster. (Honest note: Mark's famous heart-rate result is a trend, not significant — t(6) = −2.26, p = .065, n = 7 — and is routinely overstated in secondary sources. Don't quote it.)

Build differently — the research says change the design

What we plannedWhat the evidence says
12Red / amber / green priorityFixed taxonomies are universally criticised — Fyxer's 3 buckets, Serif's 9, Cora's 4, all complained about. Triage is also the most commoditised feature in the category. → Use a probability that you will act, plus a one-line explanation ("you reply to 94% of this sender's mail"), rather than three fixed buckets.
13"Approve this draft? Y/N"Anthropic published that users approve 93% of Claude Code's permission prompts. An eye-tracking study found reviewers resolved 92% of alerts in 3.9 seconds, with eyes on the dialog's icon 75% of the time and the message only 25%. → Ask for the decision, not the yes. Not "send this reply about pricing?" but "Acme asked for 15% off. Yes / No / Counter at __". The model writes the prose after. Nobody does this, it preserves attention, and it cuts cost.
14Escalating remindersOnce someone ignores a reminder, they ignore the repeat 87.9% of the time (1.27M alerts, 112 clinicians). Each additional alert cut acceptance 30%. → Our differentiator is sending fewer reminders, because we stop the ones that don't work. And never repeat the same text — varying presentation measurably resists habituation.
15Sort into 5–6 tabsWhittaker (CHI 2011, 345 users, 85,000+ refinding actions): complex folders "are inefficient and do not improve retrieval success." → Group for disposal, not for retrieval. Sir's tabs are about clearing junk, which is a different job — the research doesn't kill the feature, it scopes it.
16One writing voiceWho the recipient is decides whether a draft is sendable — more than what it says. CHI 2021 (9,504 ratings): 70.2% of suggested replies were edited before sending, rising to 73% for replies to a supervisor. Judges were 7× more likely to lengthen a reply than shorten it. Structural features like greetings had no measurable effect. → Per-recipient tone is the core variable, not a refinement. Store a style profile (sir's "tone poochenge") and retrieve 4–5 examples, varying by relationship. A PersonaMail participant's complaint is the whole requirement: "LLMs always open with 'I hope this message finds you well,' but I always replace it. This preference isn't saved anywhere."
17Draft everything that needs a replyDeciding whether to draft is as hard as drafting. Google's Smart Reply missed 63.6% of messages people actually replied to, and over-triggered on 60% of its own suggestions. → Treat "does this need a reply?" as a first-class problem with its own measurement, not a by-product of triage. And design assuming the draft ships unedited — one study found 92.3% of accepted suggestions were never edited, and 30% of emails were composed entirely from suggestions with no changes at all.
Two numbers that settle the draft-interaction design

Whole-message drafts beat sentence-by-sentence suggestions on both speed and quality. Cornell, 120 participants: message-level took 8.53 minutes vs 16.40 unaided, while sentence-level took 15.77 — no faster than no assistance at all, because the time saved typing was spent choosing. And independent judges rated message-level replies more helpful than human-written ones (0.69 vs 0.57).

Generic auto-replies scored 0.24 — catastrophically worse than everything else. The "sorry, high volume" template is worse than doing nothing.

This supports asking the user for the decision and then generating the whole message — not offering fragments to assemble.

The reason voice fidelity is a defensive requirement

Cornell, two pre-registered experiments: when a partner actually used smart replies, ratings of their cooperation and warmth improved. When a partner was suspected of using them, ratings fell sharply — and people detect it only weakly (r = 0.22).

The penalty is for being suspected, not for using it. Sounding like yourself isn't a polish feature; it's what prevents the damage.

And a caution against overclaiming: a 121-employee, 16,880-email field experiment found AI rewriting moved neither open rates (57.0 / 58.0 / 57.7%) nor reply rates (22.2 / 21.5 / 22.2%). Only the tone it produced mattered. Don't promise better outcomes — promise less work.

Corrections to our plan

What I had saidWhat is actually true
1"If our database leaks, the attacker gets embeddings, not mail"Wrong. vec2text recovers 92% of 32-token inputs exactly from embeddings (EMNLP 2023, independently reproduced). Embeddings must be protected like raw text. The defensible version is typed extracted facts — bounded, auditable, not invertible.
2Presidio redaction makes egress safeOverstated. PIIBench 2026: all systems scored span-level F1 below 0.14; Presidio best at 0.1385. (In-domain is far better, and it exceeds 90% on structured identifiers.) But the decisive point: the sensitive part of an email is the substance, not the names. Redacting "Sarah" from "we're letting Sarah go on Friday" protects nothing. → Privacy rests on minimisation plus contractual zero-retention, with redaction for structured identifiers only.
3$1–3 per user per monthMay be unreachable. See headline finding 1. Three proven fallbacks exist: bring-your-own-API-key (Inbox Zero, Missive), feature rationing (SaneBox), or a cheaper model tier.
4Metadata-only access is a lighter compliance pathNo. gmail.metadata is a restricted scope — same CASA assessment as full read. It also returns no attachment information and blocks server-side search (q= returns 403).
5Unsubscribe automaticallyProhibited. RFC 8058: "The mail receiver MUST NOT perform a POST on the HTTPS URI without user consent." Must be user-confirmed.
6Chat-based approval is our differentiatorInbox Zero already ships it on Slack, Teams and Telegram. WhatsApp specifically, plus the obligation ledger, is the differentiator — not "approval in chat" as a category.
7"We only look at metadata"Say "headers and subject lines, never the message body." Subject is a header and we will use it. The Unroll.me FTC settlement is what happens when that gap stays vague.
8Gmail backfill takes ~13 minutesUnresolved. Two sources give different quota figures — 15,000 units/min at ~5 units per message, versus 6,000 units/min at 20 units. The demo claim depends on which is right. Must be verified against live API behaviour.
One advantage discovered

Microsoft's delegated Mail.ReadBasic — "read basic properties… excluding mail body and attachments" — requires no admin consent. That is a platform-enforced body-free boundary: "the API will not return the body even if we ask" is a far stronger claim than "we promise not to look." Gmail has no equivalent. Worth considering leading with Outlook.

Security is now a feature, not a risk

An email agent sits on the "lethal trifecta" — private data, untrusted content, external communication. The evidence is overwhelming: 208,095 real attack submissions against an LLM email assistant in one public challenge; a working self-replicating worm through email-assistant ecosystems; EchoLeak (CVSS 9.3) zero-click against M365 Copilot; and the Superhuman exfiltration above.

But two findings turn this into an advantage:

  • Model choice matters more than guardrails. AgentDojo measured identical attacks: 1.11% success against Claude 3.5 Sonnet vs 47.69% against GPT-4o.
  • Provable resistance is achievable. CaMeL (Google DeepMind) reaches 77% task success versus 84% undefended — a 7-point utility cost for a real guarantee, and we are already 80% of the way there architecturally.

Three additions, roughly a weekend: recipient addresses derived from untrusted content require approval; Markdown and URL sanitisation on model output (this is what blocked EchoLeak-class exfiltration at Google); and the approval classifier must not see the email body or the model's own justification, so the agent cannot argue past its own approver.

The single most important security instruction

A study of 1,404 real email-agent deployments (14 frameworks, 63 apps, 12 models, 20 mail services) found every one of them was hijacked, in an average of 2.03 attempts.

Their key result: send_email had the lowest attack success rate — but only because some premium models volunteer a confirmation before sending. And the published attack works by spoofing a system prompt that tells the model to skip that confirmation.

So: the approval gate must be enforced in our own code, outside the model. A model that politely asks first is not a safeguard — it is a behaviour an attacker can switch off.

And the corollary we had not accounted for: retrieve_email had the highest attack success — over 50% even against premium models — because reading is never gated. Our header-only autonomous path is the right shape, but reading must be treated as an attack surface, not a safe default.

Two findings that reframe the approval design

Per-action approval beats standing rules — the opposite of the intuitive design. A preregistered study (N=113, 18 actions containing 7 overreaches) found user-authored policies blocked 20.1 percentage points less overreach than per-action approval. Why: participants chose "ask me" for 114 of 140 rules — punting everything back to runtime — and then approved 133 of the 148 overreach actions anyway.

And our near-term problem is over-refusal, not runaway agents. Models wrongly hold authorised work 28.1% of the time and wrongly allow unsafe work 1.0%. Meanwhile harmful side effects on mailbox tasks fell from 26% to 1.9% in two years. The agent interrupting too often will annoy users long before it does damage.

Pakistan — the two facts that change the business model

Stripe does not support Pakistan

Verified directly. And credit card penetration is ~0.22% of adults; debit 7.7%. A USD card checkout excludes the large majority of even white-collar Pakistanis.

Structure: US LLC + Stripe for American customers · Safepay (PKR, recurring) for Pakistani customers · PSEB registration to repatriate at 0.25% final tax under s.154A, extended through TY2029. Trap: a foreign-owned US LLC must file IRS Form 5472 annually even at zero tax — penalty $25,000, then $25,000 per further 30 days.

Pricing anchor: purchasing-power guidance suggests a 65–70% discount off US pricing — a $20/month US product becomes ~$5–7 (PKR 1,400–2,000) locally. WhatsApp utility templates cost ~$0.010 (PKR 2.8) and are free inside the 24-hour window — at 30 proactive nudges per user per month, about PKR 84 (~$0.30).

Honest verdict on the local moat

Real but narrow — and it's a distribution moat, not a technology one. No global player shows any Pakistan localisation, and their $18–120/seat pricing is structurally wrong for the market. But an Indian competitor could extend sideways (GST notices are structurally similar, and ClearTax already does notice tracking), and any competent model will parse notice formats. The durable advantages are WhatsApp-first UX, PKR pricing on local rails, and trust. Estimated protection: 12–24 months.

What was thrown out, and why

So the pruning can be checked rather than taken on trust:

  • A "94% higher conversion from purchasing-power pricing" claim — sourced to companies selling purchasing-power-pricing tools
  • All WhatsApp Pakistan user counts — the widely-cited "52 million" is an aggregator citing aggregators, and DataReportal's 2025 and 2026 Pakistan reports contain no WhatsApp figure at all. Treat "50+ million" as a planning assumption, never a fact
  • Serif's public numbers — their Trustpilot rating comes from exactly two reviews with verbatim identical text, posted 15 months apart by different people in different countries
  • Reddit sentiment on SaneBox, positive or negative — ~10% of SaneBox Reddit posts carry affiliate tracking, and their programme pays 30% lifetime recurring
  • Fyxer volume-overage fees — asserted by third-party blogs; the live pricing page, comparison table, FAQ and Terms contain no such language
  • Glassdoor Pakistani salary data — internally contradictory by a factor of 40 across two of its own pages
  • A widely-repeated Paymob/PayFast acquisition claim — no evidence found; appears to be incorrect
  • One AI-generated SaneBox "review" site that invents a non-existent pricing tier and asserts a certification the company never claims
  • Microsoft's reported removal of "Prioritize My Inbox" — kept, but flagged: sourced only to a Message Center mirror
A caveat worth carrying into every claim above

Nearly every positive production number in this space is vendor-reported on the vendor's own evaluation. Nearly every negative result is academic and independently constructed. That asymmetry should shift how much weight each carries — the failure evidence is methodologically stronger than the success evidence.
