The sentence to say while pointing at it

"An email comes in, gets taken apart by standard libraries, and falls through four deciders — our own header rules, two free date libraries, our own small model, and finally a paid model. Each one either answers or passes it along. Whatever they decide, our own code turns into one instruction to the mailbox — no model ever touches anyone's email directly.

Three of the four deciders are free. The paid one only writes replies, and only after names are stripped out. And nothing that reaches another human happens without the user approving that specific message."

Delete — we can have it, and it costs us nothing

I previously said we should skip delete entirely. That was wrong, and there's a better answer.

Gmail has two different delete operations, and people conflate them:

OperationWhat it doesPermission neededReversible?
messages.trash Moves the message to Trash — exactly what happens when a person clicks the bin icon Included in gmail.modify — the permission we already ask for ✅ Yes, for 30 days
messages.delete Destroys it immediately, bypassing Trash Needs full-mailbox access → the heavier annual audit ❌ Never
The finding

When a user says "delete this", they mean the bin icon. They expect it in Trash, recoverable, and gone after 30 days. That is messages.trash.

And messages.trash is already covered by the permission we're asking for. No extra scope, no scarier consent screen, no heavier audit, no extra cost. We can ship delete today.

The only thing we still refuse is messages.delete — instant, permanent, unrecoverable. Nobody wants that from an automated assistant, and asking for it would push us into the expensive audit tier for no benefit.

How we'd ship it safely: Trash is a separate opt-in from archiving. Default off. When on, everything trashed is reported in the daily summary, and there's a one-command "undo everything you trashed today". The 30-day window is our safety net — and it's Gmail's own, not something we built.

What to tell sir: "Delete is in. It moves mail to Trash, exactly like clicking the bin — recoverable for 30 days. It needs no extra permission and doesn't affect the audit. The only thing we don't do is permanent instant destruction, which would require full mailbox access and a much more expensive review, and isn't what anyone means by delete anyway."

scikit-learnThe priority score — simple statistics over your reply history. Fast, tiny, explainable.No language understanding.Google uses the same approach for Gmail's Priority Inbox. Proven at their scale.

The rule that chooses between them

Fixed shape → library. Needs understanding → model. Needs judgement about tone → frontier.

Rs 8,400 has a fixed shape. "by end of next week" needs understanding. "does this sound like Yahya?" needs the expensive one.

Every tool named here is open source and currently maintained. API method names are from current Gmail, Microsoft Graph and IMAP documentation. Percentages are estimates pending measurement on a real mailbox.
