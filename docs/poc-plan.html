3 · The model — what it is and why

What it is

A text classifier: an embedding model with a small classification head on top. Base is all-MiniLM-L6-v2 — 22 million parameters, about 90 MB on disk. Trained using a method called SetFit. Runs on an ordinary CPU in about 5 milliseconds per email.

What it does — exactly three jobs

  1. Two labels per email. What does the sender want from me? (asking · proposing · promising · delivering · telling) and what is it about? (meeting · money · work · account · personal · bulk). Everything downstream is decided by that pair.
  2. Which date is the real deadline, when an email contains several and the libraries can't tell which one is being asked of you.
  3. Is this a real person or automated? — the one case headers cannot settle, because transactional mail and cold sales email deliberately look personal.

Why 22 million parameters is the right size

  • It only classifies. It never writes. Sorting short text into a fixed set of labels is what encoder models are best at — this is not a hard problem for them.
  • Published benchmarks back the size. DistilBERT at 66M reaches 93–95% accuracy on classification tasks; MiniLM is faster again with only minor loss.
  • It runs on CPU in 5 ms. No graphics card, at any scale. That is what keeps the running cost near zero.
  • A larger model would cost more and add nothing for a fixed set of eleven labels.
  • It sits inside the 10–100M parameter range sir specified, and it does so because we removed writing from its job — not by compromising.

Why SetFit, and why this solves the hard part

The usual objection to training your own classifier is that you need thousands of labelled examples before it works. SetFit removes that problem.

Published result: SetFit reaches around 92.7% accuracy from 8 labelled examples per class — competitive with fully fine-tuning a model three times larger on 3,000 examples, and roughly 19× faster to train.

We have 11 labels. 8 examples each is about 90 hand-labelled emails — one afternoon of work — and it trains on a laptop.

Why not just send everything to the frontier model instead

Calls per user / monthCostPrivacy
Frontier model classifies everything~3,800~$37Every email leaves our servers
Our model classifies, frontier writes~240~$2.40Only replies leave, with names removed

How it gets better without us doing anything

  • Every time a user corrects it — reverses a filing, changes a priority — that is a new labelled example.
  • Every time it escalates to the frontier model, the frontier's answer is a new labelled example.
  • Retrain monthly. Escalations fall. Cost falls with them. The system gets cheaper as it gets older.

Why a small model is safe here

Every answer it gives is checked against the original email before we use it. If it reports a deadline of 15 September and quotes a sentence, we verify that sentence actually exists and that the date is in the future. If the check fails, the answer is discarded. Low confidence escalates rather than guesses.

Writing a reply is the only job with no automatic check — which is precisely why it is the only job we pay a frontier model for.

Do we need RAG or a vector database?

One small use. Nothing else.

UseVerdictReasoning
Finding your writing style — which 4–5 of your past emails are most like this situationYes — pgvectorNeeds similarity search. Runs inside the PostgreSQL we already have — no separate service. A few thousand vectors per user.
A vector index over the whole inboxNoMost expensive option; requires storing message content we've promised not to keep; and email tasks are thread-scoped, not corpus-scoped — replying needs that thread, not your last ten thousand emails.
Searching your mailNo — use theirsGmail and Outlook already have excellent search. We call it. Building our own would mean storing content.
Questions like "what did FBR send me?"NoA plain database query over fields we already extracted — sender, type, date. SQL, not vectors.

The same 22M model does both jobs. all-MiniLM-L6-v2 is natively an embedding model — that is its original purpose — and SetFit adds a classification head on top. So one model gives us classification and the style search. No second model, no extra cost.

And the vector store holds no content. One numeric fingerprint per sent email plus its ID. At draft time we fetch the actual text fresh from the mailbox, use it, and discard it.

Tier 4 — not built

messages.delete (instant, permanent, bypasses Trash) · changing account settings · granting access to anyone · emailing a new recipient unprompted.

These functions do not exist in our code, and we never request the permission that would allow them. Two independent barriers.

Note that delete is still available to the user — as messages.trash, which is exactly what the bin icon does, is recoverable for 30 days, and is covered by the permission we already ask for. Only instant permanent destruction is excluded, and asking for it would push us into a far more expensive annual security review for no benefit.

5 · How the user gives instructions

Everything is a plain WhatsApp message. There is no settings screen anywhere in the product. Four kinds of input, and most of them cost nothing to handle.

KindExampleHandled byCost
1. Answering us"send" · "ignore" · a tap on a buttonWord matching. No model at allFree
2. A command about what we just showed"archive that" · "remind me tomorrow"Our own 22M classifier, using the context of the last messageFree
3. A standing rule"stop reminding me two days before doctor appointments, two hours is enough"Three layers — see belowFree → pennies
4. A question"what did FBR send me?" · "what am I waiting on?"A query against our own databaseFree

Kind 3 in detail — and why it doesn't need a frontier model

A command looks like one hard problem. It is three easy ones.

Piece of the sentenceWhat it isWhat handles it
"stop reminding me… is enough"Which command — one of about tenOur existing 22M classifier
"two days" · "two hours"Time valuesduckling — already in the stack, and it reports its confidence
"doctor appointments"Which categoryOur existing 22M classifier

Assemble the three and you have the rule change. No new model, nothing extra to train.

On the parameter count: the classifier is 22M because it only sorts into buckets. The command parser is 600M because it has to produce structured output rather than pick from a list. Both run on an ordinary CPU with no graphics card — which is the constraint that actually matters.

Why a wrong parse is harmless

The model never changes anything. It proposes, and the user confirms.

You: stop reminding me 2 days before doctor appointments

Agent: Found 4 rules of type "appointment".
          Changing 2 days → 2 hours.
          Affects: doctor, dentist, physiotherapy, lab tests
          [Apply all] [Only doctor] [Cancel]

A wrong parse produces a wrong proposal, rejected with one tap. It can never produce a wrong action. That is why a small model is safe here — and why we never generalise a rule silently.

Where settings actually live

Rules are created three ways, none of them a form: sensible defaults out of the box · the user correcting something · the user saying a sentence. At runtime only the stored rules execute — no model is involved. The web dashboard merely lists your rules in plain English with a delete button. You never have to visit it.

6 · WhatsApp

WhatsApp Cloud API, direct from Meta. This is the interface — the user approves replies, gets reminders, and changes settings by sending a message.

What is required to switch it on

RequirementDetail
Meta Business accountFree to create
Business verificationSECP or firm registration · FBR NTN · a live website with a privacy policy and contact page. All must match the Meta Business Manager records exactly
A dedicated phone numberNot already registered on regular WhatsApp
Review time24 hours to 15 working days; most clear inside two weeks when documents match

This is a company paperwork task, not an engineering one, and it should start immediately — it runs in parallel with development and is the only item on this plan with an external dependency.

How we keep the cost near zero

Meta bills for messages we start. But once the user replies, a 24-hour window opens in which everything is free-form and free. So the design is:

  • One template message per user per day — "4 things need you today. Reply to start."
  • The user replies. The window opens.
  • Every approval, reminder and setting change for the rest of the day rides inside it, free.

At South Asian rates that is a fraction of a cent per user per day. Interactive buttons are supported, so Send · Edit · Ignore are taps, not typing.

9 · What is needed, and what is still unproven

What I need

  • A real cluttered mailbox to demo on
  • ~$20 for API calls during development
  • A Google Cloud project in Testing mode — free, 100 users, no review
  • Business verification started for WhatsApp — the only external dependency
  • No server, no GPU, no paid tools to begin

Must be measured in week 1 — not assumed

  • What share of a real inbox the free header rules handle. Assumed ~60%; the cost model depends on it
  • How often Presidio misses a real name. The privacy claim must not be made publicly until this is measured
  • Model accuracy on real Pakistani email, not benchmark data
One rule runs through the whole design: anything with a checkable answer is done by free tools on our own machine. Writing a reply is the only job that cannot be checked automatically, and it is the only thing we pay for.
