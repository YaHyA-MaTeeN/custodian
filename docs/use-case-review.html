Fifty use cases, one at a time:
what each means, where it argues with itself, and whether we can build it

Umar's Use Case Template v2.0, dated 9 September 2026  ·  reviewed against the code on 19 September 2026  ·  61,902 words read

Every use case below gets the same four things: what it means in plain words, the problem with it if there is one, what our system does today, and whether it can be added. The verdicts are honest about effort. A few need a decision from sir before anyone writes code, and those are marked.

The three things you noticed, checked

1 · Sending. You are right. The documents say, in bold, that the product never sends: "We never send. The Owner sends every draft from their own mail app. No exceptions, no settings, no trusted mode." (UC-25, BR-91). Outlook is connected without send permission at all (UC-05). Every forward is a draft (UC-35). A typed rule can never send (UC-38). Our system sends — after an exact typed yes, through a gate in our own code — and it was proved to work over both doors. The documents and the code disagree, and the documents also disagree with themselves: UC-26 "Send a Quick Answer" has the system send a one-tap reply from the receiving account, which BR-91 forbids two pages earlier.

2 · Deletion. Half right. He did think about it — UC-15, 16, 18 and 46 are all about clearing mail — but the rule is "the provider deletes, on the customer's own click; we never issue a deletion" (BR-59), and clearing means moving to the provider's Trash. That is exactly what our trash() does behind the typed-yes gate, and we never ask for the permission that could delete permanently. So deletion is consistent with our system. What is missing on our side is the pile: the grouped, explained, safety-checked list that the click acts on.

3 · Chat. You are right, and it is explicit. "A conversation. One sentence in, one rule out. This is not a chat assistant." (UC-38). "Parsing typed instructions is a chat assistant, which the scope guard excludes." (UC-35). Sir mandated text input; we built chat.py on exactly that instruction. This is a decision for sir and Umar, not for code. Both are buildable; they cannot both be the product.

How to read the verdicts

builtWorks today, or the remaining gap is a morning.
partlyThe pieces exist (a stage, a table, a connector method); the surface or the glue does not.
not builtNothing yet, but nothing stops it. Effort: S = a day, M = a few days, L = a week or more.
not for POCAccounts, billing, storage regions. A product company builds these; a proof of concept does not.
decisionBuildable, but it contradicts sir's instruction, another use case, or what we already proved. Needs a yes before code.

A  ·  Account and commercial — UC-01, 02, 03, 47, 48, 49

Six use cases about being a company with customers. None of them touch the pipeline. They are listed for completeness and marked not for POC.

UC-01 Register Account not for POC

Means

A visitor creates an account with email + password, or Google/Microsoft sign-in used only to prove who they are. Where their data is stored is picked silently from their country. The trial does not start yet.

Problem

None in itself. The open question at the end — which storage regions we really have servers in — drives UC-47 too.

Ours today

No accounts. The POC signs in at the keyboard.

UC-02 Sign In not for POC

Means

Four ways in, all reaching the same account. Signing in never grants mailbox access — that is a separate grant per mailbox.

Ours today

Nothing. The separation it insists on (identity ≠ mailbox access) already matches how our two doors work.

UC-03 Start a Subscription not for POC

Means

Card first, then a 7-day trial in which the scan runs but the cleanup is locked. Confirm on day 7 before any charge. Miss a renewal: stop, don't delete; 15 days to pay; then disconnect, and still nothing in the mailbox changes.

Problem

This use case gates other use cases: UC-15, 16, 17, 18 and 46 all say "clearing needs an active subscription". A POC has no subscription, so every "locked during trial" branch is moot for us.

UC-47 View or Change Where My Data Is Stored not for POC

Means

One line in settings: "Your data is stored in Europe." Move it on request; all or nothing. Cheap because we keep ~20 MB per customer, never their mail.

Ours today

One SQLite file on one laptop. The claim "moving is cheap because we hold so little" is true of our schema.

UC-48 Change Plan · UC-49 Cancel Subscription not for POC

Means

Upgrade now, downgrade at the next billing date, the billing date never moves. Cancelling runs to the end of the paid period, then disconnects, and nothing in the mailbox is undone.

Ours today

Nothing. The one rule that reaches our code — "nothing in the mailbox is reversed on the way out" — is already how my_data.py --erase behaves.

B  ·  Connecting a mailbox — UC-04 to UC-13, UC-50

UC-04 Connect Gmail by App Password built

Means

Show Google's real steps (2-Step Verification on, create an app password), strip spaces, refuse anything that isn't 16 characters before contacting Google, connect over IMAP, warn that changing the Google password kills the app password.

Problem

T-6 promises "We never delete or send your email." Our system sends over SMTP with this very password (proved: 4.9 s). Same conflict as UC-25.

Ours today

The IMAP door with the app password is the default door. The 16-character check and the "your Google password changed" pause are small additions.

Can it be added

The connection is done. The guided screen is S once there is a web sign-up.

UC-05 Connect Outlook by Microsoft Sign-In not built · M

Means

No passwords — Microsoft withdrew them. Sign in on Microsoft's page, then read the mailbox over IMAP using the sign-in token (XOAUTH2), on the same IMAP engine as everyone else. Never request send permission; replies go to Outlook's Drafts.

Problem

Deliberately no send. Same BR-91 conflict. Also note Microsoft publisher verification is needed before company accounts can connect — the document says start early.

Ours today

Nothing for Microsoft. Sir said Google only for now.

Can it be added

Yes. Our IMAP connector already speaks the protocol; it needs AUTHENTICATE XOAUTH2 instead of LOGIN, an OAuth flow for Microsoft, and the Outlook folder names in KNOWN. M.

UC-06 Yahoo · UC-07 Zoho · UC-08 iCloud partly

Means

Each is UC-04 with that provider's quirks: Yahoo hides two-step behind passwordless sign-in; Zoho needs IMAP switched on in one place and an app password made in another, has regional servers, and refuses new free accounts; iCloud's username is the part before the @ and the password looks like abcd-efgh-ijkl-mnop.

Ours today

connect.HOSTS already maps yahoo, icloud, me.com, zoho, fastmail to their IMAP servers, and imap.KNOWN has their Sent/Trash names. Untested on a real account of each.

Can it be added

Yes, S each — mostly the on-screen help and the shape checks. Zoho's regional server retry is the only real code.

UC-09 Connect Any Other IMAP Mailbox partly

Means

Work out the server from the address — MX records, a public settings list, then imap.<domain> / mail.<domain> — and ask only if all of that fails. Never accept an unverifiable certificate. Only encrypted connections.

Ours today

host_for() falls back to imap.<domain>. No MX lookup, no settings directory, no certificate policy stated.

Can it be added

Yes. Discovery chain is M. Certificate refusal is S (imaplib verifies by default; we must never pass an unverified context).

UC-10 Establish Mailbox Connection partly

Means

The shared check under every connection: prove the credential works, list folders, open the inbox read-write, find Sent/Drafts/Spam/Archive in any language, record the access level and capabilities, store the credential only after it worked, at most 2 connections per mailbox, pause (never delete) when it stops working later.

Ours today

Capability detection at connect time is exactly this idea (X-GM-EXT-1 flips flags). Spam folder found by trying names. No read-only detection, no "paused" state, no vault.

Can it be added

Yes, M. The read-only check is the SELECT reply's [READ-ONLY]; we already look at that reply.

UC-11 Connect Gmail by Google Sign-In built

Means

After Google approves us: OAuth with the narrowest scope (modify, never full), typed address pre-selected, partial grants produce a reduced service not a dead end, quota limits slow us down and never show as errors.

Problem

The document's own note: going to production unverified burns a permanent 100-user cap. And the 7-day token expiry is now measured on our side (11 → 18 Sept).

Ours today

The Gmail API door, with exactly the two scopes it asks for. Expired-token re-prompt fixed today.

UC-12 Upgrade a Mailbox to Google Sign-In partly

Means

Move a mailbox from app password to Google sign-in in place, keeping every rule and correction, re-mapping stored message ids to the new route's ids, destroying the app password only after the new connection is proven.

Ours today

The hard part exists: both doors work on one database, and connect.live_id already re-maps an id from one door to the other by Message-ID. There is no "upgrade" action.

Can it be added

Yes, S. It is a settings change plus the re-map we already do on every open.

UC-13 Disconnect a Mailbox partly

Means

End access, destroy the credential at once, erase the index on a stated schedule, and change nothing in the mailbox — labels and cleared mail stay. List pending reminders that will be lost.

Ours today

my_data.py --erase deletes our rows and leaves the mailbox alone. No per-mailbox scope, no credential vault to destroy.

UC-50 Identify the Mail Provider partly

Means

From the typed address alone: known endings go straight to their flow; unknown domains are checked by MX record (Google, Microsoft, Zoho, iCloud, other); still unknown → pick a logo.

Ours today

The known-endings half (HOSTS). No MX lookup.

Can it be added

Yes, S — one DNS query.

C  ·  The scan and the cleanup — UC-14 to UC-18, UC-46

UC-14 Scan Mailbox History built

Means

Read every envelope in bulk, never one at a time; classify from the envelope; fetch a body only where the envelope can't settle it and discard it at once; honest progress against a true count; resume, never restart; read-only.

Ours today

run.py. Bulk fetch on both doors (IMAP batched at 100 since the limits test). Resume by skipping what is held. 7,391 envelopes, no bodies. The mailbox is byte-for-byte unchanged.

UC-15 Review and Clear the Pile partly

Means

"The most dangerous use case in the system." Everything worthless, grouped by sender with a one-line reason, strongest evidence first. Five safety rules: only positive proof of bulk; never anyone you have replied to; never person-to-person; never anything that looks like a record; when unsure, keep. The provider trashes on the customer's click.

Problem

None — this is the best-written one. Note BR-147: these safety rules outrank any typed rule (UC-38).

Ours today

Every signal exists: stage 4 (bulk/unsubscribe/auto-reply), engagement_exit (never opened), store.history (replied-to), stage 5 (records). api.py /api/cleanup groups by sender. What is missing is the assembled pile with the five rules enforced and the one click that trashes through the gate.

Can it be added

Yes, M. Mostly assembly; the trash action already exists and is gated.

UC-16 Clear a Brand's Mail not built · M

Means

Recognise that a dozen addresses are one company (from DKIM / public sending records), split its mail into advertising vs records, clear the first and protect the second. Two companies on a shared bulk provider are never merged.

Ours today

Nothing brand-level. Stage 4 reads authentication results, which is where the DKIM signing domain comes from.

UC-17 Unsubscribe from a Sender built

Means

Only the machine-readable route (RFC 8058), never a link in the body. Sent from the mailbox that received the mail. Never automatic. Watch afterwards and report senders that ignored it.

Ours today

Stage 15: one-click only, refuses body links, gated as irreversible. Not built: the weeks-later "did it actually stop" check.

Can it be added

The follow-up check is S: a query over new mail from that sender.

UC-18 Reclaim Storage Space not built · S

Means

The few huge messages, grouped by sender, with how much each would free. Size alone never justifies deletion; records are protected.

Ours today

Sizes are in the index (size column; the limits test weighed all 7,882 in 7 s). No view.

UC-46 The First Cleanup not built · S once 15–18 exist

Means

The four stages in the only order that works — unsubscribe, clear the pile, brand sweep, reclaim space — one confirmation each, one result. Unsubscribe first so the mailbox does not refill while you clear it.

Problem

It depends on UC-15, 16, 17, 18. Nothing new of its own.

D  ·  New mail, every day — UC-19 to UC-24, UC-33, UC-36

UC-19 Prioritise Newly Arrived Mail built

Means

From day one: bulk or personal from the envelope, what the sender wants, first-time sender flagged, does this owner care. Every rank carries a reason. Labels are written into the mailbox. Nothing is ever hidden or moved out of the inbox by ranking. An explicit rule beats the model.

Ours today

Stages 4, 8, 9, 16; watch.py / web.py --watch; labels via sort_mailbox; reasons recorded at the moment (BR-105). Corrections checked before the model.

UC-20 Recover Important Mail from Spam partly

Means

Read the spam folder's envelopes only; score on signals spam cannot fake (you've written to them before, passed authentication, in a thread you started); surface a short list or nothing; never move anything out automatically; never load an image.

Ours today

list_spam() and rescue_from_spam() on both doors, tested. The three signals exist (store.history, read_auth, in_reply_to). No scoring pass over the spam folder.

Can it be added

Yes, S.

UC-21 Inspect a Suspicious Message Safely partly

Means

Text only, no images ever loaded, links shown as text with their true destination beside disguising text, the real sending address in full, attachments listed by name only.

Ours today

Stage 2 strips to text and the web page shows it in a <pre> — no image or remote request is made. Not built: link-text vs destination display, sender-in-full panel.

Can it be added

Yes, S.

UC-22 Track Unanswered Outgoing Mail built

Means

Only messages that asked something; a reply from anyone in the thread clears it; bulk recipients never tracked; thresholds by relationship; never chase the recipient.

Ours today

waiting.py. Gap: API door only.

UC-23 Record Commitments from Structured Mail built

Means

Embedded data first (calendar files, wallet passes, invoice XML) — no model at all. Learn a sender's layout once, relearn on redesign. Label Flights / Orders / Invoices. Body discarded after extraction.

Ours today

Stages 5 and 16; label_key applies category labels only from declared data.

UC-24 Warn About an Approaching Deadline built

Means

Warn by type of commitment, not a fixed offset; learn from how the owner reacts; ration notifications — anything not time-critical waits for the digest; several deadlines become one message.

Ours today

Stage 16 LEAD by kind, learn_from_reaction, correct.py --timing. Delivery is the terminal / web page; no notification channel.

UC-33 Identify a Request and Its Deadline partly

Means

"The use case that makes the product agentic." Read a personal message once and name what was asked, by when, and of whom. When unsure, record nothing — two humans agree only 72% of the time. Runs only on mail arriving after subscribing, never the backlog.

Problem

"Never on the backlog" contradicts our scan.py, which runs the pipeline over the backlog. Their reason is cost. Ours is that stage 9 needs history. Worth a conversation.

Ours today

Stages 7, 8, 9, 16 do "what / by when". Not built: "of whom" (owner vs sender vs third party) and the record-nothing threshold as a separate step.

Can it be added

Yes, M. It is a new output of stage 8 plus a rule.

UC-36 Resurface an Unread Important Message not built · S

Means

Something we ranked important is still unopened after ~3 days: one line in the digest, once, never repeated. Three dismissals for one sender become a correction.

Ours today

Both inputs exist (unread, decisions). One query away.

E  ·  Replying and sending — UC-25, 26, 35, 42

UC-25 Generate a Reply Draft decision

Means

Redact, draft in the owner's voice, restore names locally, write the draft into the provider's Drafts folder in the right thread from the account the message arrived at — and never, ever send. No timer, no trusted mode, no setting.

Problem

The central conflict. Everything up to the last step is what we built (stages 10, 19, 11, 12; --drafts writes to Drafts, proved on IMAP). But we also send after a typed yes, and sir's brief was an agent that "asks you before it sends anything as you" — which implies it can send. BR-91 says the product never sends under any condition. These are two different products.

Ours today

Both paths exist: draft-only (--drafts) and gated send. Switching to draft-only is deleting one branch, not building anything.

UC-26 Send a Quick Answer decision

Means

Two or three fixed one-tap answers chosen by what the message asked; the exact text is shown; the tap is the approval; the system sends it from the receiving account.

Problem

This use case sends. BR-91 in UC-25 says the product never sends. The document set contradicts itself here, and this is the page to show Umar.

Ours today

Stage 17 suggests; the send goes through the same typed-yes gate. If BR-91 wins, this becomes a one-line draft (which UC-25 ALT-3 also describes).

UC-35 Forward a Batch to Someone partly

Means

Select a set, one recipient the owner typed or picked — never an address found inside a message — review every draft on one screen, write one draft per message into Drafts, all or nothing, with an upper limit.

Problem

BR-129 is a strong rule we should adopt whatever else is decided: no address from inside an email may ever address a draft. Our user_input.py already treats email text as data, never instruction, but it does accept an address the user types in chat.

Ours today

Single forward() on both doors, gated as irreversible (it sends). Batch-as-drafts not built.

Can it be added

Yes, S: loop over create_draft with a review step.

UC-42 Tune My Writing Voice partly

Means

Show the tone recipe in plain words — "you open with Hi Sarah, sign off Best, three or four sentences, use contractions" — let the owner change it, keep separate voices for colleagues / clients / friends. An explicit setting always beats what was learned. Never show a past email.

Ours today

Stage 19 builds and describes the profile. No screen to edit it, no per-group voices.

Can it be added

Yes, S for editing; M for groups.

F  ·  Seeing your mail — UC-27, 28, 39, 40, 41, 43

UC-27 View the Unified Inbox built

Means

Every account in one list; each row shows its account; duplicates across accounts collapsed on Message-ID but remembered so an action reaches both; one button means "label" on Gmail and "move" on Yahoo; reading a message fetches it live; one provider down never blanks the screen. Not a mail client: no compose-from-scratch, no calendar, no contacts.

Ours today

inbox.py, web.py, the Next.js app. Duplicate collapse proved on a real message in four folders. Live fetch in under a second. Gap: one live connection at a time, so multi-account actions are half live.

UC-28 Search Across All Mailboxes built

Means

Search our own index — sender, subject, date, category, account — not the providers. State coverage. Whether to search inside message text is an open decision that changes the storage design.

Ours today

inbox.py --find, envelope-only, says so. BR-103 is still open, as it was.

UC-39 Catch Up After Time Away not built · M

Means

After several quiet days: four groups in a fixed order — still needs you, answered by someone else, expired, for information. Bulk counted, never listed. Nothing marked read without a click. When unsure, "still needs you".

Ours today

The inputs exist (decisions, threads, dates, unread). Needs UC-40 for "answered by someone else".

UC-40 See Where a Conversation Stands not built · M

Means

At the top of any thread of three or more: who is in it, what was asked and whether each was answered, who is waiting on whom, the latest decision. State, never a narrative summary. Each item links to its message. When unsure, participants only.

Ours today

Threads are stitched (In-Reply-To / X-GM-THRID). No state extraction.

UC-41 See Everything About One Person not built · M

Means

Link the addresses one person uses (same name and signature, replies moving between them — never on a name alone), show what you owe them and what they owe you, split a wrong link in one click. Not a contact manager: no phone numbers, no notes.

Ours today

Per-address history only.

UC-43 Work Through Today partly

Means

One ordered list: due today, promises made, requests of you, unread important, waiting on others, spam rescues. Nothing new decided here. Every item can be done, reminded later, or dismissed in place. An empty list is the goal.

Ours today

brief.py is the same idea with fewer sections; the API's /api/overview and the dashboard page are the beginning of the surface.

Can it be added

Yes, S once UC-36 and UC-45 exist.

G  ·  Control and trust — UC-29, 30, 31, 32, 34, 37, 38, 44, 45

UC-29 Correct a Decision built

Means

The reason is on the item, never behind a click. Correct in place; a correction made inside Gmail counts too; takes effect from the next message; similar past decisions are offered, never applied silently; repeated corrections are a defect in our rules, not a stubborn customer.

Ours today

correct.py, corrections table, the Gmail-removal feed on both doors (diff-based on IMAP, tested). Not built: "offer to revise similar past decisions".

UC-30 Undo an Action built

Means

Every action records the state before it; an undo is its own entry; the log covers every mailbox in plain words; trashed mail is restored via the provider; expired trash is stated plainly as unrecoverable.

Ours today

history.py and the API, one shared reverse_action, every reversal tested over IMAP today. Not built: batch undo as one unit.

UC-31 Receive the Weekly Digest partly

Means

One message on the owner's schedule covering every mailbox; silence when there is nothing; sent from our own address, never from their mailbox; "never" is a supported frequency.

Problem

"Sent from our own address" needs an outbound mail service of ours. We have none; brief.py --email sends from the owner's mailbox, which BR-91 would forbid.

Ours today

brief.py assembles the content; /api/digest too. No scheduler.

UC-32 Export or Erase My Data built

Means

Everything on our side, in a readable file, or gone — with anything kept for legal reasons named. Nothing in the mailbox changes. Refused without identity confirmation.

Ours today

my_data.py, and export in the API. Erase stays at the keyboard on purpose.

UC-34 Connect a Calendar not built · L

Means

Read today's events for the morning plan; add important events (deadlines, bookings, bills, promises) only after "Add this?" — yes; never move, change or delete an event; never email an invite; never suggest from spam or marketing. On Gmail-by-app-password the calendar needs a one-time Google sign-in for calendar only.

Ours today

We read .ics attachments (stage 5). No calendar connection of any kind.

Can it be added

Yes, but it is a new connector family (CalDAV for iCloud/Yahoo/Zoho, Google Calendar API, Graph). L.

UC-37 Set a Follow-Up Reminder partly

Means

Not snooze: the message never moves or hides. A note we keep — "mention this again on Thursday" — delivered in the digest, the owner's own words repeated verbatim, cleared if they reply first.

Ours today

The reminders table with kind remind. No way to set one on a chosen message from a surface.

Can it be added

Yes, S.

UC-38 Create a Rule in Plain English decision

Means

Type one sentence; the AI reads it once; the interpretation is shown back and must be confirmed; the rule then matches with no AI at all. A typed rule can never unlock send, delete or clear — those become approval items. Explicitly: "This is not a chat assistant."

Problem

The sentence "one sentence in, one rule out, not a conversation" is the exact opposite of sir's text-input mandate that chat.py implements. The mechanics are the same — our user_input.py already does rules-first, model-second, multiple-choice-only — but the product shape differs.

Ours today

correct.py is menu-driven; chat.py reads sentences into a fixed action set. The "show the interpretation back and confirm" loop is the missing piece and is S.

UC-44 Mark a Person as Important partly

Means

One action: never in the pile, never unsubscribed, never swept, always at the top, across every address they use. Never an alert.

Ours today

Corrections can say "always needs a reply" per sender; sensitivity marks per domain. No single "important" mark spanning all of it, and no person model (UC-41).

Can it be added

Yes, S as a correction kind that every stage consults.

UC-45 Track What You Promised not built · M

Means

The mirror of UC-33, pointed at the Sent folder: "I'll send it by Friday" becomes a promise, raised before Friday, closed when a reply in the thread carries an attachment or "here it is". Only mail sent after subscribing.

Ours today

Stage 19 reads Sent for voice; nothing extracts promises. Same machinery as UC-33 once that exists.

H  ·  UC-22 to UC-32 changed since the first set

All eleven you had before are different in this version. The changes that matter to us:

  • UC-25 now says never send, in absolute terms (BR-91 hardened), and adds the one-word draft (ALT-3) that overlaps UC-26.
  • UC-23, 24, 33, 45 now hand dates to the calendar (UC-34) — a new dependency.
  • UC-15, 16, 17, 18, 27 now carry "locked during the trial" and "read-only mailbox" branches from UC-03 and UC-10.
  • UC-29 now names UC-38 as the one allowed settings box.
  • UC-31 now lists ten sources for the digest, including rules, promises and read-only features.
